The evening light settles over the old city, and the quiet streets begin to fill with stories. In the corner of a small bookshop, a reader turns a page and pauses, listening to the rain against the glass. Knowledge, he thinks, is not a possession but a practice: something we return to, sentence by sentence, until it changes the way we see. Outside, the call to prayer rises softly above the rooftops, and the day folds itself away. Tomorrow there will be more questions, more books, and more conversations worth remembering. For now, there is only this quiet room, the smell of paper, and the patience to keep reading.
