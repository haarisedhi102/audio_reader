# an evening with ahmed javed shb

### s.momina, 2026-10-24

what im sharing below are a few answers to my questions and some other advice he gave me and my brother that I hope benefits you too.

He always says آدمی بنو (become a human being). Now with admi (man/human being) here, he doesn't mean the biological admi (of course), but an ethical being. I asked him how he would describe that in one word:

‘Someone beneficial. Once you become that, you can go on to become "religious" as we understand it. But the latter cannot and must not come before or without the former.

مفید خیر کے اصولوں پر (beneficial, upon the principles of goodness), in his words:

For friendship with Allah, what's something I should do often?

He told me, among other things, to hold on to zikr (remembrance of God), especially after every salah, every namaz. Do any zikr that Rasulullah did, anything that came out of his mouth. And he placed heavy emphasis on tasbih Fatimi

. مراقبے کی حالات میں ذکر کر نماز کے بعد۔He said:

Zikr as تخلیق ثانی (second creation), because through it, you become better every day. Because who's a man whose present isn't better than his past? And he quoted the hadith: ہلاک ہے وہ شخص جسکا آج اسکے کل سے بہتر نہیں (ruined is the person whose today is not better than his yesterday).

He once (a few years ago) spoke a little harshly about independent learning. I, back then, had no teachers as such. That sentiment remained with me, and I asked him to elaborate on that. I can't transcribe all of it, but here's the gist.

Among other things, he mentioned that mere information gathering is not knowledge, because true knowledge is transformative. Knowledge requires company, صحبت (companionship), and ادب (etiquette/respect) with and of teachers. But in the absence of such, one must continue to learn independently, but with a critical view of one's opinions and findings.

Once something is formed, an opinion or an understanding, it must be checked by an expert in that area and field. One must continuously strive to find good teachers. Find an expert and check your understanding, because it happens that your claim is right but your way of coming to it, or proving it to someone else if you have to, is wrong. An expert will help you identify and correct those mistakes and areas.

I shared how I learn, and how Western scholarship and (Western-trained) scholars are an indispensable part of my learning at the moment, for which he said پیاس ان سے بڑھائیں، پانی اپنے کوؤیں کا پیےں (increase your thirst through them, but drink the water of your own well), meaning use those resources to spark curiosity and ask or find questions, but your ultimate loyalty should lie with your own tradition.

I asked him about یکسوئی (single-minded devotion), again something he talks a lot about. Below is a summary of what I remember:

Our یکسوئی to Allah is like a circle, a دائرہ . It's not a linear line where a person only sees Allah and becomes blind to everything else. That is reserved for the very close and very special friends of Allah. As for the rest of us, we operate in a circle, and everything in that circle is guided by our یکسانیت (consistency/uniformity) towards Allah and our وفاداری (loyalty). Even a good design is عبادت. And so is learning history, art, and more. All of this stems, and must stem, from our belief, love, and connection with Allah.

Perhaps on the day of judgement, the "apparently" non-religious actions will weigh the heaviest on your meezan (scale). Like paying off someone's debt and never allowing them to know. This isn't meant to downplay the importance of obligatory actions and apparently religious actions, but rather to shed light on the dichotomy that we so firmly believe in, that of ethics and religion. Where going to the masjid every day to sit in the first row is important, but doing anything to help your distressed neighbour is not.

And hence, he said that your private life, your baatin should be 100% better than your public life. And this, I think, is perhaps among the most important things he said. This makes or breaks a man, a man who's trying to become a مومن اور مومنہ).

He said: سچا آدمی اپنی خوبیوں کو رازوں کی طرح چھپاتا ہے

Then my brother, who was with me, realised that he could be comfortable with him and that he wouldn't be judged or looked down upon for the lack of a beard on his face. Below are some of the things he advised him.

To my 17 y/o brother:

"Protect yourself from the گھٹیاپن (lowliness/baseness) of your desires. Stay away from everything whose end is dunya (ہر وہ چیز جسکا مقصد دنیا ہے) (everything whose purpose is worldly life). Your desires, cravings, خواہشات (desires) determine who you truly are."

خواہشات کی سطح اونچی ہونی چاہیے کہ۔ جو دنیا کو مقصود بناتی ہوں ان پر قابو رکھیں

In your age, there just are certain sins/mistakes that you'll be guilty of, that you'll do. Just don't let the distance between repentance and sin become big. Hold on to this.

Your relations with people should be guided by ایثار، انکساری اور اخفاء۔ (selflessness, humility, and concealment [of one's good deeds]).

All of this doesn't do justice to the things discussed and is in a lot of ways incomplete. For a better understanding of all of these concepts, one is advised to watch his videos on YouTube. And take from him all the Khair.
